Feeling Lost in Life? I Get It. Here's Where I Started

If you don't know what's next, you're not broken. Here's where I started.

By Tammey

For four years now, I've lived alone. It's the first time in my whole life that I've only had me to take care of.

I was the oldest. The mama hen. I took care of my siblings, then my mom, then my own four kids, then a husband who needed me as his caregiver. So when there was finally no one left to look after, you'd think I'd feel free.

I didn't. I felt lost.

If you've typed "feeling lost in life" into a search bar, I want you to know something right away: I haven't figured it all out. I'm building it as I go. But I've learned a few things about the first steps, and I'd love to share them with you.

What feeling lost really looked like for me

It wasn't dramatic. Nobody would have looked at me and known.

I went to work. I came home. The house was quiet. My creativity, the thing that had always been my escape, was just gone.

So there I was. Alone. Lonely. No purpose I could name. Some days it felt like one great big bucket of pain and loneliness, and I didn't know how to set it down.

Maybe yours looks different. Maybe the kids are grown and the house is too quiet. Maybe you retired from a job that was your whole identity. Maybe you're still surrounded by people and somehow feel lost anyway. However you got here, if you're asking "what now?" and nothing answers back, I get it. I've been there. Some days I'm still there.

When you've always been the one taking care of everyone

Here's something I didn't see coming. When you spend your whole life caring for other people, that becomes who you are. You know what to do when someone needs you. You're good at it.

But when nobody needs you anymore, the question isn't just "what do I do now?" It's "who am I now?"

I didn't have an answer. And honestly, even as a trained life coach, I couldn't coach my way out of it. Knowing the right questions doesn't help much when you don't have the energy to answer them.

The day I found out my bucket was empty

After a while, I thought, maybe I should do something creative. Maybe that would help. I had a friend who loved journaling as much as I do, so we held a small journaling workshop. Just five people.

It went well! People loved it. They wanted more.

And I couldn't give it to them. I was exhausted for weeks afterward. I was so shocked. It was five people! I used to teach scrapbooking online every week to a community of over 1,500 people.

That's when I understood: my bucket was empty. I had nothing left to pour. I'd been trying to find my purpose by giving, because giving was all I knew, and there was nothing left in me to give.

If you're tired all the time and you can't figure out what you want, this might be you too. You're not lazy. You're not broken. You might just be empty. And you can't find your purpose on an empty tank.

What helped me take the first steps

None of these are big. That's the point. When you feel lost, big plans just add to the pile. These are the small things that slowly started filling my bucket back up.

1. A simple routine

When nothing feels certain, a few things you can count on help. I started small, with a handful of things I did every day no matter how I felt. Nothing fancy. It gave my days a shape again.

2. Learning to be quiet

I learned to meditate, and I learned about sound therapy too. For someone who'd always been busy taking care of somebody, quiet was new. Those quiet minutes became the place where I started hearing myself again.

3. Paying attention to what I ate

I started learning about real food and steering clear of the boxed stuff. I'm not going to tell you what to eat. I'll just say that taking care of my body was one of the first ways I started taking care of me.

4. Talking to someone

This one matters most, so I'll say it plainly: I worked with a counselor, and it helped.

I almost didn't go. Years ago, after my first husband left, I tried counseling and it didn't help at all. So I wasn't expecting much. But this time I got lucky. He was just what I needed and nothing like what I expected.

If you tried once and it wasn't a fit, please don't let that be the end of it. The right person can make all the difference.

5. One small creative thing

Not a workshop. Not a project. Just something small for me. A few lines in a journal. A photo of something that catches your eye. If you want somewhere to start, morning pages are a gentle one, and so is gratitude journaling.

If you're feeling lost right now

Here's what I'd tell you if we were sitting at my kitchen table with a cup of tea.

  • You're not broken. Feeling lost after a big change, or a long season of giving, makes sense.
  • Fill your bucket before you go looking for your purpose. Rest isn't quitting. It's the first step. My self-care plan guide can help you start.
  • Pick one small thing. Not five. One. Do it for a week.
  • Don't do it alone. A counselor, a friend, a group. Having someone in your corner is a big part of what keeps you going.

And if feeling lost has turned into feeling hopeless, please reach out today. In the US, you can call or text 988 any time. You don't have to be in a crisis to call.

I'm still building it

I once heard that the best thing you can share with others is something you've conquered yourself. I haven't conquered this yet. But I'm conquering it, one small step at a time, and I'd rather walk next to you than shout back from the finish line.

That's what Inspiration to Unstuck is for. If you'd like some company on the way, come say hello in our Facebook group, or reach out to me. I'd love to hear where you are.

You're not alone in this. I promise.

Tammey
