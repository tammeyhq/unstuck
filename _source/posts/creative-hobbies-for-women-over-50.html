For a long time, I didn't make anything just for me.

Life got so overwhelming that I couldn't seem to function anymore. I took care of what needed done and not much more. Some days, a shower was more than I could manage.

Creating something for fun wasn't even on the list.

My creative well is still refilling

I love to write, but my creative well is pretty empty right now. I'm working on healing and letting it fill back up, slowly.

I can't force it. I tried and I think it made it worse.

Instead, I'm starting small. I rearrange a shelf and appreciate the joy it brings me.

Hobbies that are helping me find my way back

None of these takes talent. Trust me on that.

Coloring

I bought markers and a large, beautiful coloring book. Someone else already did the hard part of drawing the lines. All I have to decide is the color.

I leave it on a shelf in the dining room. This makes it easy to grab when inspiration hits and easy to put away when I'm done.

Playing with AI

This one might surprise you. I brainstorm with AI about things I could create and how to do them. And sometimes I just ask it to make pictures that make me laugh.

Sometimes I ask it to teach me things. It does!

Making my home feel pretty again

Right now I'm decluttering my life and my home. As I go, I'm replacing old, worn-out things with pretty ones that bring me joy.

That counts as creative, too. I get to choose the colors, textures and little touches that make me happy.

Painting (even if you're terrible at it)

I went to one of those paint-and-sip parties once. We were painting a simple pine tree on a lake shore in summer.

Mine turned out to be a green triangle.

It was so bad that the artist teaching the class came over to rescue me. She turned my painting into a winter scene so she could add snow to the tree and give it some branches!

I still laugh about it, and it's hanging in my house as a reminder that life isn't perfect and joy is about the journey.

The paint party wasn't about making a masterpiece. I didn't think I'd be that miserable at it, but I still had a great time and we all laughed at our mistakes.

It was all about an evening of trying something new with other people who are figuring it out too.

Family history

One area that still really pulls in my attention is my family history. I LOVE sitting at Ancestry.com, connecting people and finding documents!

Photography

I love photography. You don't need a fancy camera to start. The phone in your pocket works just fine. A photo-a-day challenge is a gentle way to begin.

It reminds you to see your surroundings and to take time to stop and appreciate what is around you. It's about the ridiculously fat little bee buzzing in the flowers, the stunning dragonfly with wings of iridescent purple and blue, the sweet furkid on your lap, or the delicious meal you made because you wanted to.

Writing and journaling

I love to write. It's the one I'm waiting on. My well isn't full enough yet, and that's ok.

If writing calls to you, a prompt can be the spark that gets you going. That's why I made my 100 Creative Prompts journal, with a page to write on for every prompt.

A few more ideas to try

  • Gardening, even a few pots on the porch
  • Knitting or crochet
  • A beginner pottery class
  • Handmade cards
  • Collage or a vision board
  • Cooking something new, just because it sounds fun

Start small, and start for you

Pick one thing. Give it ten minutes. Don't worry about how it turns out.

You might end up with a green triangle. That's ok. Somebody might even add snow.

Nobody's grading this. It's just for you.

We're all figuring things out as we go.
