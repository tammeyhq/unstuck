For four years now, I've lived alone. It's the first time in my whole life that I've only had me to take care of.

I was the oldest. The mama hen. I took care of my siblings, then my mom, then my own four kids, then a husband who needed me as his caregiver. So when there was finally no one left to look after, you'd think I'd feel free.

I didn't. I felt lost.

I have a feeling there are a lot of us out here feeling this way. Maybe you're one of them? If so, pull up a chair. I don't have it all figured out. I'm building it as I go. But I can tell you where I've been, and what helped me take the first few steps.

What feeling lost really looked like for me

It wasn't dramatic. Nobody would have looked at me and known.

I went to work. I came home. The house was quiet. My creativity, the thing that had always been my escape, was just gone.

So there I was. Alone. Lonely. Broken. No purpose I could name. Some days it felt like one great big bucket of pain and loneliness, and I didn't know how to set it down.

Maybe yours looks different. Maybe the kids are grown and the house is too quiet. Maybe you retired from a job that was your whole identity. Maybe you're still surrounded by people and somehow feel lost anyway.

Take a minute with that. Where are you right now? Not where you think you should be. Where you actually are.

Wherever that is, I get it. I've been there. Some days I'm still there.

When you've always been the one taking care of everyone

Here's something I didn't see coming. When you spend your whole life caring for other people, that becomes who you are. You know what to do when someone needs you. You're good at it.

But when nobody needs you anymore, the question isn't just "what do I do now?" It's "who am I now?"

I didn't have an answer. And honestly, even as a trained life coach, I couldn't coach my way out of it. Knowing the right questions doesn't help much when you don't have the energy to answer them.

The day I found out my bucket was empty

After a while, I thought, maybe I should do something creative. Maybe that would help. I had a friend who loved journaling as much as I do, so we held a small journaling workshop. Just five people.

It went well! People loved it. They wanted more.

And I couldn't give it to them. I was exhausted for weeks afterward. I was so shocked. It was five people! I used to teach scrapbooking online every week to a community of over 1,500.

That's when I understood: my bucket was empty. I'd been trying to find my purpose by giving, because giving was all I knew. And there was nothing left in me to give.

If you're tired all the time and you can't figure out what you want, I wonder if this might be you too. You're not lazy. You're not broken. You might just be empty. And none of us can find our purpose on an empty tank.

So the next step isn't finding your purpose. Not yet. The next step is filling your bucket.

How I started filling my bucket back up

None of these are big. When you feel lost, big plans just add to the pile. These are the small, real things that helped me.

1. Making time for me (even when it felt silly)

Let me be honest. Taking time for myself felt selfish. Silly. Wasteful, even. Why? What did it matter? Who was it for?

I'd read articles about self-care and scoff. Easy for them to suggest spa days with friends or weekends away. That was not going to happen.

So instead, I booked my first pedicure. That's it. Small. Easy. Not for anyone else. Just for me.

What would your pedicure be? Not the grand gesture. The small, easy thing that's only for you.

2. Taking one day at a time

I stopped trying to figure out the rest of my life. That was too big, and it made everything feel heavier. I just focused on today. A handful of things I did every day, no matter how I felt. Even one standing appointment every Wednesday at 2 gave my week something to hold on to.

3. Learning to be quiet again

I learned to meditate, and I learned about sound therapy too. For someone who'd always been busy taking care of somebody, quiet was new. But those quiet minutes became the place where I started hearing myself again.

If the idea of meditating feels strange or out of reach, I understand. I'll be writing more about what it is and how to start, gently.

4. Finding my inner voice

This, along with listening to my body, made the biggest difference of all.

All my life, I'd relied on that quiet inner voice. That knowing. And somewhere along the way, I'd lost it. I didn't realize it was missing until it was so far gone I barely remembered it. I had no idea when I'd lost it.

Have you ever had that feeling? That you used to just know things, and now you second-guess everything?

So I went looking for it. My faith carried me. I pray. I listen. Day by day, that voice is coming back. I'm still working on it, but it's getting better.

5. Listening to my body

Our bodies tell us so much, if we'll stop long enough to hear them. I started learning about real food and steering clear of the boxed stuff. And I started paying attention. If I go to eat something and feel any negative reaction at all, I simply don't eat it.

I'm not going to tell you what to eat. You know your body better than anyone. I'll just say that listening to my body was one of the first ways I started trusting myself again.

6. One small creative thing

Not a workshop. Not a project. Just something small for me. A few lines in a journal. A photo of something that catches your eye. If you want somewhere to start, morning pages are a gentle one, and so is gratitude journaling.

If you're feeling lost right now

Here's what I'd tell you if we were sitting at my kitchen table with a cup of tea.

  • You're not broken. Feeling lost after a big change, or a long season of giving, makes sense.
  • Fill your bucket before you go looking for your purpose. Rest isn't quitting. It's the first step. My self-care plan guide can help you start.
  • Pick one small thing. Not five. One. Your version of a pedicure.
  • Listen to yourself. You know more than you think. Your body and your inner voice have been trying to tell you something. Give them a quiet minute to be heard.
  • Find your people. A friend, your faith community, a group of women who get it. Having someone in your corner is a big part of what keeps you going.

And if feeling lost has turned into feeling hopeless, please reach out today. In the US, you can call or text 988 any time. You don't have to be in a crisis to call.
